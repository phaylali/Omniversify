# Siege of Mazagan (1562)

The **Great Siege of Mazagan (1562)** — fought **18 February to 7 May 1562** — was the most famous of the nine sieges the Portuguese star fortress endured and the severest test of Benedetto da Ravenna's 1541–48 trace.

After Morocco's unification under **Mohammed al-Shaykh (1549)** and his son **Abdallah al-Ghalib (from 1557)**, the **Saadi Sultanate** targeted Mazagan, the anchor of Portugal's southern Morocco. Through 1560–61 Governor **Álvaro de Carvalho** was in Lisbon; his deputy **Rui de Sousa de Carvalho** sent a spy to Fez, then a distress brig to Regent Dona Catarina. On **18 February 1562** the *qaid* of Azemmour arrived with an *alfaqueque* demanding surrender. Rui de Sousa de Carvalho replied: *"May he come as fast as he can, because I had been waiting for him in this fortress with plenty of quinces, pears and pomegranates and other such fruits which were not lacking in this fortress."*

## Prelude

Saadian preparations ran through 1560–61 after the fall of **Santa Cruz do Cabo Guer (1541)** and the evacuations of **Safim and Azamor (Oct 1541)** had left Mazagan as Portugal's sole southern square. The 1541–42 superfortress — 275 × 200 m rectangle, five bastions, 12 m thick scarps and buxas-fed ditch — was now a decade old and fully manned. Portuguese intelligence from Fez confirmed the coming *mhalla*.

---

## The Siege

The **Saadian host** invested the land front, entrenching before the **Governor's Bastion and the south curtain** and bringing **24 cannon** to batter the scarps. Assaults concentrated on the **Governor's and Santo António bastions** and the **Traitor's Gate (D)** antechamber. Relief — a patriotic wave of volunteers from Portugal, including fidalgos who paid their own passage — entered via the **Sea Gate (Porta da Ribeira, L) and the calheta harbour** between the Angel bastion and the sea curtain, keeping the 2,800 inside supplied.

The **69 embrasures** and the **seawater moat (20 m × 3 m, ~2 m held at low tide by the S/T buxas)** did their work: frontal fire from curtains and grazing (rasante) fire from orillon-protected flanks swept the ditch, while the covered way (Q) and the Revelim (P) before the Porta da Vila (A) broke the first column. After roughly ten weeks the besiegers, thinned by disease and Portuguese artillery, **withdrew 1 May**; the formal lifting is dated **7 May 1562**.

## Aftermath

The victory — **nine sieges in 255 years, the 1562 the hardest-fought** — confirmed the Renaissance bastioned system's value and was celebrated in Lisbon as a patriotic triumph. The fortress was repaired, its ditch cleared (the 1611 Codex Cadaval plan records a later cleaning), and the garrison rotated under **Álvaro de Carvalho de novo and Rui de Sousa de Carvalho de novo** (1562, then 1564–72). The Saadians turned to internecine struggle; Mazagão would hold until the **final siege of 1769**.
